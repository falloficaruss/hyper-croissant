Features

Everything You Need to Understand Chess

Not just another chess GUI. Oropis bridges the gap between engine analysis and human understanding.

Chess Intelligence Layer

Zero-AI symbolic analysis extracts position features, identifies tactical motifs, maps strategic concepts, and detects plans — all pure chess logic before any LLM is involved.

AI Coach Mode

Interactive coaching where the AI asks you questions before revealing answers. Like working with a human coach who adapts to your level.

Eval Swing Explainer

Don't just see "blunder." Understand exactly why your move lost 2 pawns — pinned knight, weakened square, opened file for the opponent.

"Why Not" Comparison

Compare your move vs the engine's best move side-by-side with structured explanations of what you lost and what the engine preserved.

Multi-Engine Support

Run Stockfish, Leela Chess Zero, or any UCI-compatible engine. Built-in download manager gets you running in seconds.

Game Library

Import games from Lichess, Chess.com, or PGN files. SQLite-backed personal database with search, filter, and full metadata.

Explanation Levels

Explanations That Match Your Level

The same position, explained four different ways.

Beginner < 1200 ELO

"This move attacks two pieces at the same time. The knight on f5 threatens the rook on h6 and the pawn on d4. Your opponent can only save one of them, so you win material."

Intermediate 1200–1800 ELO

"Nf5 creates a powerful outpost on f5, supported by the e4 pawn. The knight targets the weak h6 and d6 squares while creating a tactical double attack. Black cannot challenge this outpost because the g-pawn has already advanced."

Advanced 1800–2200 ELO

"Nf5 exploits Black's inability to contest the dark squares after …g6. The knight dominates f5 with no viable trade, creating a permanent positional advantage. Combined with the half-open g-file and Black's weakened kingside, White has a lasting initiative targeting h6 and d6."

Master 2200+ ELO

"After Nf5, the knight establishes permanent dominance on the dark-square complex. Black cannot play …g6 without weakening h6 further, and …Be6 allows Nxd6. The plan is Qd2-h6, doubling on the g-file with Rg1, while the knight restricts …Bf8 defensive resources. The position is strategically winning; Black's bishop pair is neutralized by the closed center."
